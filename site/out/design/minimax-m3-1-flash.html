The night line

One night,
four worlds

The dune crossing is not a gap between two places — it is the journey itself. Four landscapes, one sleeper train, and the slow change of light.

  1. 21:40

    Salt Flats

    Boarding under sodium lamps. Blankets, coffee, the whistle and a platform of sleepers.

  2. 01:15

    Ashfall Dunes

    The lights dim to ember. The desert takes the train, and every window becomes a planet.

  3. 09:40

    Cinder Pass

    Sunrise over black glass mountains. The whole carriage falls quiet without a word.

  4. 14:00

    Neon City

    Platform One. Steam, rain on hot asphalt, and a skyline that never turned it off.

Why the night train

Built for
the in-between

Three things we refused to compromise on: where you lie, what you eat at 3 a.m., and how you arrive.

01

Every window faces the desert

Cabins sit on the outer wall of the train, so the dunes roll past at eye level and the sunrise arrives in your room — curtain still drawn.

Outward-facing berths
02

The Lantern Car never closes

Broth at 3 a.m., coffee at six, cardamom buns at seven. The kitchen runs the whole length of the night, and the steward remembers your order.

Open 24 hours
03

You arrive, not just disembark

A hot shower, a pressed shirt and a quiet lounge wait at Platform One — breakfast, a phone to borrow, and the city at its gentlest.

Arrival lounge included
Timetable

Four departures
a night

Trains leave the salt flats at dusk. The last one pulls out at half past midnight, when the desert is at its coldest and its quietest.
