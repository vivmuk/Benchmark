Board at dusk.

Every evening the Dune Line pulls out of Amber Basin with its lamps low, runs eleven quiet hours through open sand, and delivers you to breakfast under the signs of Neon Harbor. No clouds, no airports — just 1,204 kilometres of the oldest landscape on record sliding past a window.

01

The Glass LookoutCar 3 · swivel seating · dimmed at 22:00

Half this carriage is a window. Its arched glass runs clean over the ceiling, so when the sun drops behind the western ridges the whole desert turns rose-glass for about nineteen minutes. Sand foxes are reported most nights around kilometre 640. Lantern policy: candle-temperature light only.

02

The Caravan KitchenSingle seating · 22:00 sharp · lamp-lit

A four-course desert menu cooked from what actually grows along the line — saltbush, tepary beans, dates off the oasis oasts. The chef presents every course with the kilometre it was dreamed up at, so dinner becomes a kind of slow地图 of the route. Coffee is served exactly as the moon clears the plateau.

03

Starlight BerthsCars 6–8 · ceilings fold open to the sky

Every cabin ceiling folds back to bare sky; there is no glass between the mattress and the Milky Way, only waxed canvas if you insist. Wool mattresses, cedar-lined drawers, and a porter who wakes you twenty minutes before Neon Harbor — never earlier.
