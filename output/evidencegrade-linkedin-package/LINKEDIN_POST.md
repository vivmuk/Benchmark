# LinkedIn Post — EvidenceGrade: from citations to confidence

**AI systems in healthcare should not merely cite evidence. They should make the *strength* and *limits* of that evidence visible.**

OpenEvidence’s **EvidenceGrade** is an attempt to do that in real time for applicable clinical AI answers.

Rather than treating every citation as equivalent, the system describes a GRADE-inspired workflow that evaluates retrieved papers across three dimensions:

• **Quality** — is the study design appropriate for the question?  
• **Certainty** — how reliable and precise are the findings?  
• **Relevance** — how directly does the evidence apply to the clinical claim?

It then considers the body of evidence as a whole and returns a grade from **A to D**, with modifiers, or **U: Unable to Grade** when a responsible assessment cannot be made.

That last category is important. In clinical decision support, visible uncertainty is more useful than false precision.

But a real-time evidence grade is not a final verdict. Its value depends on what was retrieved, how well relevance is judged for the actual patient and setting, whether guidance is current, and whether the grading is independently calibrated against expert clinical-methodology review.

The useful question is not: *Does the AI have citations?*  
It is: *Can I see how strong the evidence is—and why?*
